Tri-Modal-ECG: an 800,000-study multimodal ECG dataset built on MIMIC-IV-ECG with  1

scan-like image augmentation, multi-stage quality control, and three reproducible  2

benchmarks for cross-modal verification, temporal grounding, and physiological     3

plausibility                                                                       4

Neeraj Choudary1, Banoth Rohith Rathod1, and Anshika Mishra1                       5

1Mahindra University, Hyderabad, India                                             6

Abstract                                                                           7

The electrocardiogram (ECG) is a primary, non-invasive modality for assessing cardiac electro- 8

physiology, and machine learning systems for ECG analysis are increasingly multimodal: they 9

consume waveforms, ECG-paper images, and free-text reports together. The recently released 10

MEETI dataset showed the value of pairing MIMIC-IV-ECG signals with rendered images, beat- 11

level features, and large-language-model interpretations1. MEETI, however, is constrained on 12

three axes that limit benchmarking: it provides plotted images for only roughly 10,000 records, 13

its annotations are produced by GPT-4o and therefore depend on a closed model, and it does 14

not partition the underlying 784,680 records into reproducible train/validation/test splits or expose 15

downstream tasks. To address these constraints we present Tri-Modal-ECG, a fully open and 16

reproducible multimodal ECG resource that covers 799,706 quality-controlled studies from 17

161,328 unique patients drawn from MIMIC-IV-ECG. Each record is aligned across four modal- 18

ities: a Lead II waveform array, a 12-lead waveform array, a clean ECG-paper PNG rendering, 19

a deterministic scan-like JPG simulating clinical document degradation, the original machine- 20

generated text report, and the corresponding structured measurements (heart rate, P/QRS/T 21

timing, axes). On top of this core resource, we release three large benchmark task datasets gen- 22

erated entirely by deterministic, audit-able rules without any LLM in the loop: Task A (cross-modal 23

verification, 5,597,755 rows), Task B (per-second temporal grounding using local R-peak detection 24

and QRS delineation, 2,399,118 rows) and Task C (physiological-plausibility classification, 4,726,480 25

rows). All splits are patient-level with verified zero patient overlap between train, validation and 26

test, eliminating the leakage modes that confound study-level random splits. We document a 27

multi-stage quality-control pipeline (PIL+OpenCV image integrity audit, waveform length and 28

decoder validity, non-empty report check) whose persisted JSON artefacts let any user audit 29

the dataset's 400,000+ files without re-running the build. Compared with MEETI, Tri-Modal-ECG 30

covers  80× more images, adds a scan-like image stream, exposes deterministic benchmark 31

tasks, and provides a verifiable patient-level split. We hope this resource lowers the barrier 32

for reproducible multimodal ECG research, and supports rigorous evaluation of cross-modal 33

alignment, temporal localization, and grounded reasoning over cardiac signals.     34

Background & Summary                                                               35

Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of mortality worldwide2, and the 12-lead ECG 36

remains the primary non-invasive tool for diagnosing arrhythmias, conduction disorders, and 37
ischemia3­5. Modern ECG analysis is increasingly multimodal: clinicians and machine learning 38

systems consume the waveform itself (as digital samples), the rendered ECG paper image, 39

and the cardiologist's textual report together, and recent ECG­language models extend this 40
fusion further6­9. Vision­language pre-training paradigms originally developed for natural images 41
and pathology10­13 have been transferred to ECG, but their evaluation has been bottle-necked 42
by datasets: most public ECG corpora provide a single modality (waveforms, e.g., PTB-XL14, 43

                                        1
ICBEB15, SHDB-AF16), or two modalities (waveform + report, e.g., MIMIC-IV-ECG17; image + 44

report, e.g., common clinical archives), forcing ECG­language models to be evaluated through 45

bespoke pipelines.                                46

The MEETI release1 is, to our knowledge, the first attempt at a four-component MIMIC-IV-ECG 47

extension: signal, image, beat-level FeatureDB parameters, and an LLM-generated interpretation 48

per record. Tri-Modal-ECG inherits MEETI's central insight--that multimodal ECG research needs 49

a single corpus where signal, image, structured features and free text are aligned by a common 50

identifier--and addresses three practical limits we observed when trying to build a benchmark on 51

top of MEETI:                                     52

1. Image coverage. MEETI1 releases approximately 10,000 ECG paper images out of 784,680 53
    records (Section "Usage Notes"). Tri-Modal-ECG renders 800,033 clean ECG-paper PNGs 54
    and 800,033 scan-like JPGs from the full record list and ships both as part of the dataset. 55

2. LLM-free annotations. MEETI's per-study textual interpretations are produced by GPT-4o18, 56

which makes faithful reproduction difficult: the GPT-4o variant available at any future date 57

may differ, the prompt sensitivity of LLM annotations is well documented19, and downstream 58

evaluations cannot easily separate "model is wrong" from "annotation was wrong". Tri-Modal- 59

ECG generates all benchmark labels deterministically from the structured measurement 60

table and from explicit signal-processing logic on Lead II (NeuroKit220 R-peak detection21 61

and wavelet-based QRS delineation22), so every label is reproducible by re-running our 62

scripts.                                          63

3. Benchmarks and splits. MEETI does not expose train/validation/test splits or task defini- 64

tions on top of the four-modality records. Tri-Modal-ECG provides a patient-level 80/10/10 65

split with explicit leakage checks, and three benchmark tasks (verification, temporal ground- 66

ing, plausibility) totalling 12,723,353 rows.     67

A second contribution is the scan-like image stream. Many clinical ECGs reach hospital 68

archives only as scans or photos of paper output; a model that has only seen pixel-perfect plots 69

will degrade when deployed against scanned documents. We therefore release a deterministic 70

OpenCV-based augmentation that, seeded by study_id, rotates, applies a small perspective 71

skew, blurs, and JPEG-compresses every clean PNG, producing one matched scan-like JPG per 72

study. The transform is fully deterministic so the augmented image of a given record is identical 73

across re-builds, and the per-study seed eliminates the train/test sneak-leak risk that random 74

augmentations carry.                              75

A third contribution is the multi-stage QC pipeline. Producing 2.4M files (Lead II .npy, 76

12-lead .npy, clean PNG, scan-like JPG, and per-study QC JSON for 800K studies) without any 77

single decoder failure is a non-trivial engineering target. We track validity at three levels: signal 78

(waveform decodes and length equals 5,000 samples), image (PIL verify + OpenCV decode 79

for both modalities), and text (non-empty machine report). Per-study QC JSONs and a final 80

dataset_integrity_summary.json are persisted on disk so a downstream user can audit our 81

coverage claims without rerunning the build.      82

Finally, we expose three benchmark tasks that match how multimodal ECG models are actually 83

trained and evaluated. Task A: Cross-modal Verification treats a record as the triple (signal, 84

image, text) and constructs structured negatives--random swaps of a single modality, partial 85

mismatches, and same-heart-rate-bucket hard negatives--to evaluate alignment beyond trivial 86

cues. Task B: Temporal Grounding returns ten per-second binary labels for each of three queries 87

(HIGH_HEART_RATE, LOW_HEART_RATE, WIDE_QRS) computed locally from R-peak detection and QRS 88

delineation; the labels are non-uniform within studies (Figure 16, Table 1) and therefore carry a 89

real temporal signal. Task C: Physiological Plausibility assigns SUPPORTED, CONTRADICTED, 90

                                               2
or UNCERTAIN to eight short physiological claims for every study, allowing both natural-language 91

entailment and grounded-reasoning evaluation against structured ECG measurements.                92

Master index and patient-level split                                                                106

We build a master index by concatenating report_0. . . report_17 into a single T_machine text 107

field per study, joined to the waveform path and subject_id, and persisted as a single Apache 108

Parquet file28. Splits are constructed at the patient level: unique subject_ids are shuffled with 109

NumPy24 seed 42 and partitioned 80/10/10 into train, validation, and test subsets; every study 110

inherits the split of its patient. We then verify that the three patient sets are pairwise disjoint, 111

eliminating the dominant leakage mode for ECG datasets in which the same patient contributes 112

multiple recordings (Figure 4, Table 7).                                                            113

Clean ECG image rendering                                                                          124

Each Lead II array is rendered to a clean ECG-paper PNG with Matplotlib25 using fixed parameters: 125

figure size 12×2.5 in, time axis 0­10 s, y-range [-2, 2], light ECG-paper background colour #fffafa, 126

major x grid every 0.2 s and minor every 0.04 s, major y every 0.5 and minor every 0.1. Amplitude 127

is normalised by dividing the signal by its 99th-percentile absolute amplitude and scaling by 1.2, 128

which clips outliers but preserves morphology (Figure 7). Studies for which the 99th-percentile 129

amplitude is exactly zero (a flatline or fully saturated record) are detected, logged, and excluded 130

rather than producing a meaningless image. Workers use Matplotlib's headless Agg backend; we 131

leave two CPU cores free during rendering so the host system remains responsive.                   132

Scan-like image generation                                                                    133

Real-world ECG images often reach a model as scans or photos of paper, not as pixel-perfect 134

plots. We therefore release a second image stream: a scan-like JPG produced from each clean 135

PNG by a deterministic OpenCV26 augmentation chain. For each study we (i) seed a NumPy 136

generator from the integer study_id, (ii) downscale the width to at most 1,600 pixels, (iii) rotate by 137

±2, (iv) apply a small perspective shift sampled in [-1%, +1%] of width, (v) Gaussian-blur with 138

kernel size 3 or 5, (vi) jitter brightness and contrast, and (vii) JPEG-encode with quality in [70, 95]. 139

Figure 8 shows the six-step chain on one example, and Figure 9 shows clean / scan-like pairs 140

across multiple physiological conditions. Because the seed depends on study_id and not on 141

a wallclock or worker index, the augmented JPG of a given record is bit-for-bit identical across 142

rebuilds, which protects downstream evaluations against silent drift.                         143

The three benchmark tasks                                                                      175

Task A: cross-modal verification                                                               176

Task A asks: do this signal, this image, and this text belong to the same ECG study? For each 177

cleaned study we emit two positive rows (image_type=clean and image_type=scanlike) and 178

up to five negative rows: TEXT_SWAP, IMAGE_SWAP, SIGNAL_SWAP, PARTIAL_MISMATCH (image and 179

text from another study, signal from anchor), and a same-heart-rate-bucket HARD_NEGATIVE_TEXT 180

(text swapped from a study in the same HR bucket; only emitted if a same-bucket alternative 181

exists). HR buckets are computed from rr_interval as brady (< 60 bpm), normal_low (60­80), 182

normal_high (80­100), and tachy ( 100); see Figure 14 for an end-to-end illustration of the 183

same-bucket text swap. Figure 13 walks through the full row-construction logic on a real anchor 184

study, and Figure 15 reports the final negative-type counts.                                   185

Local detection logic. An earlier baseline of Task B used the global rr_interval to set all ten 193
bins to either 0 or 1, which carried no temporal signal. We replaced that logic with per-bin local 194
detection backed by NeuroKit220. Specifically: (i) R-peak positions are detected on the full Lead II 195
signal with the Pan-Tompkins algorithm21; (ii) instantaneous HR is computed at the midpoint 196
of each detected RR interval as 60 · FS/RRsamples and per-bin HR is the mean of the midpoints 197
inside that bin (with nearest-midpoint fallback for empty bins); (iii) QRS onsets and offsets are 198
produced beat-by-beat by NeuroKit2's wavelet delineator (ecg_delineate, method dwt) following 199
the Martínez et al. approach22, and a bin is labelled 1 if any beat with QRS onset inside that bin 200
has duration > 60 samples (=120 ms). When R-peak detection fails we fall back to the global 201
rr_interval rule for HR queries (and zeros for WIDE_QRS, which cannot be inferred without QRS 202

boundaries); the QC-cleaned index makes such fallbacks rare.                                             203

Usage Notes                                                                    355

Authoritative entry point. The single source of truth for the dataset is processed/index/index_ 356

clean_with_measurements.parquet. Every benchmark task is generated from this index, and 357

every per-study QC JSON is keyed by the same study_id. Users who only need a tabular 358

interface to the cleaned dataset can ignore the JSONL files entirely.          359

Splits. Splits in our parquet and JSONL files are patient-level. Do not re-shuffle records at 360

the row level; doing so silently introduces patient leakage, which has been shown to inflate ECG 361

model performance by up to several AUC points on PTB-XL-style corpora29,30.    362

Image modality choice. Models that ingest only the clean PNG will be exposed to a more 363

uniform pixel distribution than they will see in a deployed setting. We strongly recommend training 364

(or at least evaluating) on the scan-like JPG; the deterministic seed makes this reproducible and 365

safe to combine with the patient-level split.                                  366

Sentinel codes. The structured-measurement columns inherit MIMIC's sentinel codes (29999, 367

32767, 65535, -32768, 0) for missing values. Apply the physiological-plausibility filters de- 368

scribed in Section "Distribution of structured measurements" before computing summary statistics. 369

The benchmark-task generators apply task-specific handling: Task A uses HR buckets that map 370

invalid RR to "no bucket"; Task C emits an UNCERTAIN row when the relevant measurement is 371

missing.                                                                       372

Local detection vs. global stamps. Task B's labels are produced by NeuroKit220 R-peak 373

detection21 and wavelet QRS delineation22; the global RR-based fallback only fires when local 374

detection raises an exception. Users who want the all-bins-equal global rule for ablations can 375

recompute it from the structured measurements; users who want stricter local detection can 376

replace NeuroKit2 with their preferred pipeline by editing scripts/task_B.py.  377

No LLM in the loop. All benchmark labels are deterministic functions of the structured 378

measurements and Lead II signal. There is no GPT-4o or similar LLM call in the data pipeline; this 379

is the main reason the benchmark tasks are reproducible across rebuilds and across hardware. 380

Limitations. (1) Task B's R-peak detector inherits the failure modes of Pan-Tompkins on 381

extremely noisy signals; the QC pipeline removes flatline records but cannot remove every noisy 382

beat. The global fallback is conservative on WIDE_QRS (always-zero), which slightly underesti- 383

mates wide-QRS prevalence in noisy studies. (2) Task C uses MIMIC's record-level machine 384

measurements rather than per-beat measurements; deeper investigations of beat-level variability 385

(e.g., heart-rate variability, dynamic QT prolongation) are better served by the per-beat FeatureDB 386

extraction shipped with MEETI1. (3) Both release-time runtime numbers and intermediate failure 387

counts are persisted only for the scan-like generation pass; runtimes for clean rendering, QC, and 388

                                               26
the three task generators are printed to the console but not durably logged. (4) The released 389

audit-md notes minor schema drift between this paper's JSONL keys (labels, claim, label) and 390

an earlier rule-book (y_bins, claim_tag, y_support); we have prioritised the schemas as actually 391

generated, and downstream tooling should follow Tables 4­6.  392
